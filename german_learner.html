Step 1 Foundation

Alphabet, Umlauts & Phonetic Sound Laws

German is largely phonetic: words sound exactly as they are spelled once you master the foundational sound rules below. Click any letter to hear native pronunciation.

V V sounds like English "F"

In native German words, V is pronounced /fau/ (like "fow"). Example: der Vater (father) is pronounced "FAH-ter", vier (four) is "FEER".

W W sounds like English "V"

German has no "wuh" sound. The letter W is always pronounced like the English "V". Example: das Wasser (water) sounds like "VAH-ser", wo (where) is "VOH".

Z Z sounds like "TS" in "Cats"

German Z is never a buzz. It is a crisp, sharp /tset/ like the "ts" in "tsunami" or "cats". Example: die Zeit (time) sounds like "TSYTE", das Ziel (goal) is "TSEEL".

Step 5 Vocabulary Core

Noun Mastery & Der / Die / Das Gender Gym

Every German noun is capitalized and carries a gender. Always learn the noun together with its article: der (M), die (F), das (N).

💡 Pro Suffix Rule (Instant Gender Detection):

Nouns ending in -ung (die Erfahrung, die Entscheidung), -heit/-keit (die Gelegenheit), -schaft (die Teamarbeit), -tion (die Situation) are 100% Feminine (die)! Nouns with prefix Ge- and ending in -nis (das Ergebnis) or verbal infinitives (das Leben, das Essen, das Wissen) are typically Neuter (das).

Step 8 Syntax & Connectors

Conjunctions & The "Verb-Kicking" Subordinate Clause Rule

Coordinating conjunctions (Position 0) keep standard S-V-O word order. Subordinating conjunctions (weil, wenn, obwohl, nachdem) push the conjugated verb to the very end of the clause!
